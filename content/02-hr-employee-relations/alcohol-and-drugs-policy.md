## 1. Statement of Intent

Nudge Education Ltd works with children and young people, many of whom are vulnerable. Staff must be fit to carry out their duties safely and with sound judgement at all times. Alcohol and drug misuse puts learners, colleagues and the public at risk, damages the trust placed in us by families and commissioners, and harms the person misusing.

This policy has two aims that sit together. It states clearly what is prohibited and how breaches are dealt with. It also commits the organisation to treating dependency as a health matter first, and to supporting any member of staff who asks for help before a problem affects their work or anyone's safety.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, workers, contractors, sub-contractors, agency staff, volunteers and directors of Nudge Education Ltd, across every provision including Nudge Education Online (NEO). It applies wherever work is carried out: in a learner's home, a community venue, a Nudge office, a vehicle used for work, online, or at any work-related event or social occasion.

It covers alcohol; illegal drugs (controlled substances under the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971); psychoactive substances (so-called "legal highs") under the Psychoactive Substances Act 2016; solvents and other substances used for intoxication; and prescribed or over-the-counter medication where it affects a person's ability to work safely.

## 3. Legal Framework

This policy is written with regard to the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 (the employer's duty to provide a safe workplace and the employee's duty not to endanger themselves or others); the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971; the Psychoactive Substances Act 2016; the Road Traffic Act 1988 (driving while unfit through drink or drugs); the Equality Act 2010 (noting that addiction to alcohol, nicotine or any other substance is excluded from the definition of disability by the Equality Act 2010 (Disability) Regulations 2010, but that conditions arising from or alongside dependency may themselves be disabilities); the Employment Rights Act 1996; the Rehabilitation of Offenders Act 1974 as modified for work with children; and Keeping Children Safe in Education (current edition) and the Managing Allegations framework, which apply where a member of staff's conduct indicates they may be unsuitable to work with children.

## 4. Rules

Staff must not report for work, carry out any work, or be in contact with a learner, family or commissioner while under the influence of alcohol or drugs, or while their performance or judgement is impaired by any substance. "Under the influence" means that a reasonable observer would conclude that the person's behaviour, speech, coordination, judgement or appearance has been affected; it does not depend on a measured level.

Staff must not consume alcohol or take illegal or psychoactive substances during the working day, including breaks, or at any point before work where the effect will persist into working hours.

Staff must not possess, use, supply, offer to supply, manufacture or be concerned in the supply of illegal or psychoactive substances in any work context, on any work premises, in any work vehicle, or on any work device or platform.

Staff who drive as part of their role, or who transport learners, must never do so while over the legal limit or while unfit through drink or drugs, and must not have consumed alcohol on the day before driving a learner. A conviction for driving while unfit is a safeguarding matter as well as a conduct matter.

Staff must not consume alcohol with, in front of, or in the presence of a learner, and must not use alcohol or drugs in any way that could be seen, heard or inferred by a learner, including on camera or audio during online delivery, in a shared living space during a video call, or through social media visible to learners or families.

Moderate alcohol consumption at an organised work social event where no learners are present and no further work or driving follows is permitted. Staff remain subject to the Code of Conduct at such events and are expected to behave in a way that would not embarrass the organisation or breach professional boundaries. Alcohol is never provided at events where learners or families are present.

Staff who are taking prescribed or over-the-counter medication that may affect alertness, judgement, coordination or mood must check the guidance supplied with the medication, follow it, and tell their line manager (without needing to disclose the underlying condition) where it could affect their ability to work safely, drive, or supervise learners. The organisation will consider reasonable adjustments. Taking prescribed medication as directed is not a breach of this policy.

## 5. Support and Self-Referral

Any member of staff who believes they have, or may be developing, a dependency on alcohol or drugs is encouraged to tell their line manager or HR / People as early as possible. A member of staff who comes forward voluntarily, before their conduct has given rise to a disciplinary or safeguarding concern, will be treated as having a health matter and supported, not disciplined, for the disclosure itself.

Support may include referral to the GP or occupational health, access to the employee assistance scheme or external services (for example the NHS, Alcohol Change UK, FRANK, We Are With You), time off for treatment under the Sickness Absence Policy, temporary adjustment of duties, and a structured return. The organisation will agree a written support plan with the person and review it regularly. Information about the dependency is special category health data and will be held confidentially under the Data Protection and Information Security Policy, shared only with those who need to know to provide support or keep learners safe.

Support does not displace safeguarding. If a disclosure indicates that a learner has been placed at risk, the Designated Safeguarding Lead must be informed and the Managing Allegations Against Staff procedure may apply alongside the support route. Self-referral also does not protect a person from the consequences of a subsequent breach of the rules in section 4, or of a refusal to engage with agreed support.

A member of staff who is suspected of having a dependency but has not come forward will be approached by their line manager in a private, non-judgemental conversation and offered the same route. If the person denies a problem and conduct or performance concerns continue, those concerns will be managed under the Disciplinary and Investigation Policy or the Performance Management Policy as appropriate.

## 6. Where a Breach Is Suspected

### 6.1 What happens on the day

The trigger is any reasonable sign that a colleague may be under the influence or has recently used alcohol or drugs: the smell of alcohol or cannabis on their breath, clothing or in their vehicle or room; slurred or unusually loud speech; unsteadiness; glazed or bloodshot eyes; unusual drowsiness, agitation or disinhibition; or a report from a learner, parent, carer, colleague or commissioner. A smell alone, with no sign of impairment, is still a concern to be acted on and recorded, because the rules in section 4 prohibit recent use in a work context and not only visible intoxication, and because cannabis is a controlled substance.

Whoever notices the sign acts, regardless of seniority. The steps are the same across Nudge Education and NEO:

1. **Keep learners safe first.** The colleague does not start, or stops, any contact with a learner, family or commissioner. In a learner's home or a community venue, the practitioner withdraws and the session is ended or covered; online, the session is ended or handed to cover and the colleague leaves the call. No explanation is given to the learner beyond that the session has had to change.
2. **Tell the right person immediately.** At Nudge Education, the colleague's line manager or, if unavailable, the duty senior leader. At NEO, the Director of NEO and Head of School (DSL) or the Deputy DSL. If the concern is about the manager or DSL themselves, use the escalation map in the Managing Allegations Against Staff Policy.
3. **Do not let them drive.** If the colleague has driven to work or is due to drive, the manager arranges safe transport home. If they insist on driving while apparently unfit, the manager tells them the police will be informed, and does so.
4. **Have a short, private, factual conversation.** The manager tells the colleague what has been observed, asks whether there is an explanation (medication, illness, a legitimate reason for a smell such as a spillage), and tells them they are being sent home while the matter is looked into. The manager does not diagnose, argue or accuse. The colleague is not asked to self-test.
5. **Record it the same day.** The person who observed the sign and the manager each write a dated, factual account of what was seen, smelt or heard, by whom, when and where, and what the colleague said. Opinion and speculation are left out. At NEO, the observer completes the Low Level Concern form (link below). At Nudge Education, the account is sent to the line manager and HR / People, and to the DSL where a learner was present or affected.
6. **Decide the route the next working day.** HR / People, with the DSL where a learner was involved, decide whether the matter is a low-level concern (a single sign, no impairment, credible explanation, no learner affected), a disciplinary matter (impairment, or possession or use of illegal substances in a work context, or a learner exposed), a support matter under section 5 (the colleague discloses a dependency), or more than one of these at once. The colleague is told the outcome of that decision and what happens next.

The two provisions differ only in who receives the report and how it is recorded:

| | Nudge Education (face-to-face, home and community) | NEO (online) |
|---|---|---|
| First report to | Line manager; duty senior leader if unavailable | Director of NEO and Head of School (DSL); Deputy DSL if unavailable |
| Record | Written account to line manager and HR / People same day | [NEO Low Level Concern form](https://script.google.com/a/macros/nudgeeducation.online/s/AKfycbzD_pxo1IsIvK5QkuOuHXLHBmABA1HiLCC-_I65RzALeBaSDQp7kjyhQhbDkZVF-ZvL/exec) (nudgeeducation.online sign-in required), same day |
| Learner present or affected | DSL informed same day; parent or carer and commissioner informed by the DSL as appropriate | DSL informed by the form; parent or carer and commissioner informed by the DSL as appropriate |
| Immediate cover | Session ended; line manager arranges cover for subsequent sessions | Session ended or handed to cover; Head of School arranges cover |
| Most likely source of the report | Parent, carer or learner, because practitioners work alone | Colleague, learner or parent on a live call; recording may form part of the record |

Because Nudge Education practitioners work alone in learners' homes, a parent or carer is often the first to notice. Any report from a family about a practitioner's apparent state is treated as a concern under this section from the moment it is received, is never dismissed as a complaint about the service, and is passed to the line manager and DSL the same day.

### 6.2 Low-level concerns and patterns

Where the matter is treated as a low-level concern, it is handled under section 4.2 (Nudge Education) or section 6 (NEO) of the Managing Allegations Against Staff Policy: addressed with the colleague in a supportive but clear conversation, recorded on the low-level concerns log, and reviewed by the DSL termly for patterns. Two or more concerns of this kind about the same person within a term are treated as a conduct matter under the Disciplinary and Investigation Policy, and may be referred to the LADO where a learner may have been put at risk.

### 6.3 Testing

Nudge Education does not operate routine or random alcohol or drug testing. The organisation reserves the right to ask a member of staff to undergo a test, with consent, where there are reasonable grounds and where a test would materially assist an investigation; a refusal may be taken into account but will not of itself be treated as proof of a breach. Decisions on testing will be made by HR / People with a director, never by a line manager alone.

### 6.4 Investigation and disciplinary action

Investigation and any disciplinary action will follow the Disciplinary and Investigation Policy. Being under the influence of alcohol or drugs at work, and possession, use or supply of illegal or psychoactive substances in any work context, are listed in that policy as gross misconduct and may result in summary dismissal. Dependency disclosed only after a breach has occurred will be taken into account as a factor but does not prevent disciplinary action.

Colleagues who are concerned that a member of staff may be working while impaired must report it as set out in 6.1. Reporting in good faith is protected under the Whistleblowing Policy. Covering for a colleague who is impaired, or failing to report a concern that a learner may be at risk, is itself a conduct matter.

## 7. Criminal Offences and Declarations

All staff must tell their line manager or HR / People without delay if they are investigated, arrested, charged, cautioned or convicted in relation to any drug or alcohol offence, whether or not it occurred at work, including drink- or drug-driving. This duty is set out in the Practitioner Code of Conduct and in the Recruitment, Selection and Sub-contracting Policy, and applies for the duration of employment. Failure to declare is itself a disciplinary matter and may be more serious than the offence.

The organisation will consider each declaration individually, taking into account the nature of the offence, its relevance to work with children and vulnerable young people, any driving or safeguarding implications, and the person's openness. Where the matter indicates the person may be unsuitable to work with children, it will be referred under the Managing Allegations Against Staff Policy to the Local Authority Designated Officer. Where the organisation dismisses or removes a person from regulated activity because they have harmed, or pose a risk of harm to, a child, it will make a referral to the Disclosure and Barring Service as required by law.

Nudge Education will cooperate with the police and will not obstruct any lawful investigation. Illegal substances found on work premises or in work vehicles will be handed to the police.

## 8. Roles and Responsibilities

The CEO holds overall accountability for this policy. HR / People owns it, advises managers, decides on testing and leads support plans. Line managers apply the rules, hold the private conversation when they are concerned, act immediately where a breach is suspected, and record what they observe. The Designated Safeguarding Lead (for NEO, the Director of NEO and Head of School, or the Deputy DSL) is informed whenever a learner may have been affected and decides on any safeguarding action. All staff are responsible for their own fitness for work, for following this policy, for declaring medication and criminal matters where required, and for reporting concerns about colleagues.

## 9. Confidentiality and Records

Records relating to support plans, medication disclosures and investigations under this policy are held by HR / People as special category or criminal-offence data under the Data Protection and Information Security Policy and the Staff Privacy Policy. They are retained in line with the organisation's retention schedule and are not placed on general personnel files or shared surfaces.

## 10. Monitoring and Review

HR / People reviews this policy annually and following any incident, change in law or change in guidance. The number of incidents and self-referrals (not names) is reported to the directors annually.

## NEO Online Addendum

Nudge Education Online staff work from home or from other private locations and deliver through screens, audio and chat. The rules above apply in full. In addition: a member of staff who is visibly or audibly under the influence on a live lesson, mentoring session or recorded call is treated as being under the influence in front of a learner, and the session recording (used only for safeguarding and training purposes) may form part of the investigation. Alcohol, drug paraphernalia or evidence of use must not be visible in the camera background during any contact with learners or families. Where a Practitioner-Mentor or Qualified Educator is unfit to deliver a scheduled session, they must say so in time for cover to be arranged rather than attempt the session. The Director of NEO and Head of School acts as DSL for these purposes and will decide whether a concern is a conduct matter, a safeguarding matter or both.
