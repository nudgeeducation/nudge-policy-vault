*A fully online alternative provision for learners aged 11–18*

## 1. Basis

The Prevent duty (Counter-Terrorism and Security Act 2015, section 26) and the *Prevent duty guidance: England and Wales* (2023, in force 31 December 2023) require specified authorities, including schools and registered childcare providers, to have due regard to the need to prevent people from being drawn into terrorism, and to assess the risk of that happening in their setting. NEO is not a registered school and is not a specified authority in its own right. NEO adopts the duty in full because Keeping Children Safe in Education 2026 makes Prevent part of safeguarding, because the online education standards (4.1) expect it, and because an online provision serving young people who have been out of school is exactly the setting in which online radicalisation can go unseen. The Prevent provisions are set out in the [[../03-service-delivery/child-protection-and-safeguarding-policy|Child Protection and Safeguarding Policy]]; this document is the risk assessment and action plan that policy requires.

## 4. Referral route

A concern that a learner may be susceptible to radicalisation is a safeguarding concern. Staff report it to the DSL the same day using the concern form. The DSL decides, with the Head of School where appropriate, whether to refer to the local authority's Prevent lead or make a Channel referral, and whether a referral to children's social care or the police is also required. The learner's commissioner is informed where the placement is commissioned. The DSL records the concern, the decision and the reasons.
