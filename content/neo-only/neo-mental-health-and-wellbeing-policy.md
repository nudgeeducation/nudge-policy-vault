*A fully online alternative provision for learners aged 11–18*

## 1. Purpose

Most learners come to NEO because school stopped working for them: anxiety, illness, exclusion, a placement breakdown, or long periods out of education. Wellbeing is not an add-on to NEO's curriculum; it is the condition for it. This policy sets out how NEO notices, measures and supports learner mental health and wellbeing, what it does when a learner is struggling, where its role ends and clinical services begin, and how it protects the wellbeing of the adults who do this work. It gives effect to the mental health and trauma expectations in Keeping Children Safe in Education 2026 and the online education standards (4.1), and draws on the DfE's *Mental health and behaviour in schools* and *Promoting and supporting mental health and wellbeing in schools and colleges* guidance, applied by NEO as its standard.

## 2. The Cornerstones

NEO's model is built on six Cornerstones: Connection, Movement, Creativity, Reflection, Rest and Nutrition. They shape the day (a 10:00 start because attendance rises when anxious mornings are given room; short lessons with breaks; water, snacks and moving about as part of how we teach; Friday Cornerstones Day) and they are the frame in which wellbeing is noticed and talked about with learners, without clinical language. The Cornerstones apply to staff as well as learners (section 7).

## 3. Noticing and measuring

Every learner completes the WHO-5 Wellbeing Index at the Initial Assessment and at the start and end of each half-term, with their Practitioner-Mentor, used unadapted so that scores are comparable over time. Scores are held on the learner's record, discussed with the learner and, with their agreement, the family, and reported to the NEO board only as cohort movement, never by learner. A low or falling score is a prompt for a conversation and a support-plan review, not a diagnosis. Daily noticing happens in the 09:30 mentor group and the 13:00 check-in: the Practitioner-Mentor is the one adult who sees the learner every timetabled day and is expected to act on what they see.

## 4. Support at NEO

Support is graduated. Universal: the Cornerstones, the RSHE and Relational Intelligence curriculum, a named Practitioner-Mentor, small groups, cameras-off as a regulation strategy, a breakout room with the mentor available in every session, and the "Tell someone" routes in the [[neo-privacy-notice-learners|NEO — Privacy Notice for Learners]]. Targeted: a support plan agreed with the learner, family and SENCo (adjusted timetable, check-in frequency, sensory and screen adjustments, work in Google Classroom when live sessions are too much), reviewed each half-term. Specialist: where a learner needs more than an education setting can provide, the DSL and SENCo work with the family, the commissioner and the home local authority to secure it (GP, CAMHS or the local mental health support team, the school nurse, social care), and NEO keeps the learner's education going alongside.

NEO staff are not clinicians and do not provide therapy or counselling. They notice, listen, keep the learner safe, and connect them to help.

## 5. When a learner is at risk

Any indication that a learner may be at risk of harm, including self-harm, suicidal thoughts, disordered eating or abuse, is a safeguarding matter and is reported to the DSL the same day under the [[../03-service-delivery/child-protection-and-safeguarding-policy|Child Protection and Safeguarding Policy]]. The DSL is contactable throughout live session hours. Where a learner is in immediate danger during a session, the host keeps the learner on the call, the co-host contacts the DSL and the family, and emergency services are called if the learner cannot be reached or is not safe; the critical incident protocol applies. Staff do not ask safety-assessment questions; they stay with the learner and get the right adult. Smoothwall Monitor alerts on managed devices are triaged by the DSL under the online safety policy.

## 6. Families and commissioners

Families are told how NEO supports wellbeing at induction and can raise concerns at any time through the Practitioner-Mentor or the Tuesday coffee morning. Weekly reports to families and commissioners include a plain-language wellbeing note. Information about a learner's mental health is shared only as the [[neo-data-protection-confidentiality-and-privacy-policy|NEO — Data Protection, Confidentiality and Privacy Policy]] allows: to keep the learner safe, with the learner's involvement wherever possible.

## 7. Staff wellbeing

NEO is a small, distributed team doing emotionally demanding work. Planning time is protected in contracts; Friday reflective practice is asynchronous for those who do not work Fridays; supervision for the DSL is monthly and for Practitioner-Mentors termly; half-termly face-to-face meet-ups are funded; an employee assistance route is available through Nudge Education; and the half-term reset means regular meetings relax and leave is taken in breaks. The rule in NEO's own AI policy, that systems must know how to stop, applies to people first.

## 8. Roles

| **Role** | **Responsibility** |
|---|---|
| Designated Safeguarding Lead | Owns this policy with the SENCo; leads the response to risk; liaises with clinical and statutory services. |
| SENCo | Support plans; WHO-5 cadence; adjustments; links to EHCP reviews. |
| Director, NEO & Head of School | Reports cohort wellbeing to the board; protects staff wellbeing arrangements. |
| Practitioner-Mentors | Daily noticing; WHO-5 with the learner; first conversation; same-day escalation. |
| Qualified Educators | Teach to the Cornerstones; raise concerns the same day. |
| Families and commissioners | Share what NEO needs to know; act on referrals NEO cannot make itself. |
